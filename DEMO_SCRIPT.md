# Jezero Expedition Intelligence (JEI) — 2-Minute Evaluator Demo Script

**NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026** — *Challenge 11: Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map*  
**Presenter:** Martian Sapiens Team  
**Target Duration:** ~120 seconds  
**Live URL / Preview:** `http://localhost:5173` (or Render deployment)

---

## Act 1: The Landing & Mission Framing (0:00 – 0:25)

**Action:** Open the web app. The dark graphite UI loads immediately with Jezero Crater's HiRISE orthomosaic centered.

**Narrative:**
> *"Welcome to Jezero Expedition Intelligence (JEI). When human astronauts and surface rovers explore Mars, conventional maps fail them by providing either vague planetary overviews or dangerous 'black box' scores that hide real risks.*  
> *JEI is an evidence-first science atlas and traverse planner designed for Jezero Crater. Looking at the bottom status bar, notice our coordinate reference frame: Mars 2000 planetocentric, equirectangular, with an honest basemap caveat indicating approximate alignment at 20 meters per pixel."*

---

## Act 2: Science Target T7 & Scientific Integrity (0:25 – 0:55)

**Action:** Click **T7** (Cheyava Falls / Bright Angel) in the left target rail or directly on the map.

**Narrative:**
> *"Let's select Target T7: Cheyava Falls at Bright Angel, where Perseverance discovered organic-associated reaction rims—often sensationalized in the media.*  
> *Notice our Right Panel. We never make unsubstantiated claims. Look at the biosignature assessment chip: it reads unequivocally: **'Potential biosignature — not confirmed evidence of life.'**  
> *Scroll through the evidence feed. Alongside the PIXL and SHERLOC detection claims, JEI displays the Scheller et al. 2024 counter-evidence right by default: highlighting how cerium-3 luminescence can mimic organic fluorescence through purely abiotic mineral mechanisms. Every claim is linked directly to peer-reviewed literature with clickable DOIs."*

---

## Act 3: Objective-Based Reordering (0:55 – 1:15)

**Action:** In the TopBar, switch the **Mission Objective** from *Biosignature* to *Accessibility*, then to *Geological History*.

**Narrative:**
> *"JEI does not use a single composite score. Instead, mission planners choose their operational objective. Under 'Biosignature', T7 ranks first. But if we switch to 'Accessibility', Target T1 at Octavia E. Butler Landing and the flat crater floor take priority, while rugged rim traverses (T8 and T9) drop down due to steep slopes. The underlying four assessment cards never change—only our operational priorities do."*

---

## Act 4: Route R1 & Elevation Profile (1:15 – 1:35)

**Action:** Click the **Route R1** toggle button in the TopBar.

**Narrative:**
> *"Now we activate Science Traverse R1. This renders the 23.3-kilometer traverse path connecting T1 to T7, complete with an interactive elevation profile derived from HiRISE DTM sampling.*  
> *Notice our prominent warning banner: **'Rover traverse segment. Not a human-rated route.'**  
> *Scrubbing along the profile, we see +452 meters of gross ascent and apparent grades up to 29.9%. JEI transparently discloses that this includes high-frequency dune ripple topography, comparing it with a smoothed human-scale baseline of ~301 meters and net rise of +215 meters."*

---

## Act 5: EVA Sortie Walk-Back Model & Environmental Caveats (1:35 – 2:00)

**Action:** Click **EVA Sortie** in the TopBar. Adjust the *EVA Duration* slider to 5 hours, or change the *Anchor* to T4. Then click **Environment** in the TopBar.

**Narrative:**
> *"Finally, we activate the EVA Sortie Planning Model. Anchoring at our current habitat or rover position, JEI calculates the human operational walk-back limit based on life-support consumables, suited walking speed, and terrain slope penalties using IAU Mars geodesy ($R = 3,396,190\text{ m}$). The dynamic golden boundary shows exactly which science targets are reachable.*  
> *Opening the Environment drawer, you see our five operational cards, with non-negotiable disclosures: radiation is clearly labeled as an analogue from Curiosity's Gale Crater measurements, and present-day shallow ice is noted as not established at Jezero.*  
> *Jezero Expedition Intelligence gives explorers the truth of the Martian surface: rigorous science, transparent caveats, and operational clarity."*
